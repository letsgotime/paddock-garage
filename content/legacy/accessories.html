Where to start looking

Tesery (tesery.com) is a dedicated Tesla accessories store that's been running since 2021, with a Model Y catalog split by generation, so it's easy to find parts sized for a legacy car instead of a Juniper one. It's one option, not the only one. Tesla's own shop (shop.tesla.com) sells OEM parts at OEM prices with guaranteed fit. Teslarati's shop is another Tesla-focused storefront worth comparing against before you buy. None of this is an endorsement of one seller over another. Treat it as a starting point for comparison shopping.

What owners buy first

Roughly in the order most owners buy them, based on what comes up most in owner forums and shows up as bestsellers at the stores above:

  • All-weather floor liners: the single most common first purchase. The factory carpet has no protection at all, and liners trimmed to the Model Y footwell keep road salt and mud off it.
  • Frunk and trunk organizers: the car ships with no cargo organization anywhere, front or back. Fitted bins stop groceries and gear from sliding around both trunks.
  • Center console tray: the console's open storage tunnel is deep but unshaped, so small items slide around and disappear into it. A fitted insert fixes that.
  • Windshield and rear sunshades: closer to necessary than optional on a car with this much glass overhead. Interior temperatures climb fast without one.
  • Center screen protector: the 15.4" display handles nearly every control in the car, so it takes constant touch contact. A matte or glass protector is cheap insurance against scratches.
Skip anything that changes the exterior lighting, badging, or aero pieces if resale matters. Those are the easiest changes for a future buyer to flag as not stock, and the cheapest to leave alone.
