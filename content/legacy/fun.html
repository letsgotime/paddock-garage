Gone from your car specifically

The Atari arcade classics (Missile Command, Asteroids, Lunar Lander and the rest) were removed in June 2024 specifically from vehicles with AMD Ryzen infotainment. Your 2024 Model Y almost certainly has that hardware, so don't go looking for them; the current Arcade lineup rotates other titles (Cuphead, Beach Buggy Racing 2, Stardew Valley among them).

Also worth correcting: light-up, auto-presenting door handles are a popular aftermarket accessory, not something Tesla actually ships from the factory. If you've seen that on another car, it wasn't stock software.

Things experienced owners find embarrassingly late

  • Trip Planner auto-preconditioning: navigating to a Supercharger automatically warms the battery pack so it hits peak charge speed the moment you plug in, estimated to save up to 10 minutes per stop. A December 2025 update added live stall occupancy to the map.
  • Tesla Theater streaming (Netflix, YouTube, Disney+, Hulu, Twitch): requires the car fully parked with the parking brake set: it does not work while driving, and doesn't turn on just because Autopilot or FSD is engaged, despite that being a common assumption.
  • In-car web browser: a real browser in the app dock, useful at a Supercharger stop. A 2026 update added full-screen support and even browser-based video calling.
  • Naming your car in the app isn't just cosmetic: certain names (Monty Python and Hitchhiker's Guide references among them) trigger their own hidden responses.
Not yet on this car, for what it's worth: the newest door-open "TESLA" wordmark animation that debuted in a mid-2026 software update is limited to 2024+ Model 3 and the redesigned Juniper Model Y. Something to look forward to if you ever upgrade, not something missing from your current software.
