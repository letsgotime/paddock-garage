Wheels

Long Range AWD offered exactly two wheel choices in 2024, no 18" or 21" option on this trim (21" Überturbine wheels are Performance-only):

SizeNameTireCost
19"Gemini Wheels255/45R19Standard
20"Induction Wheels255/40R20Paid upgrade (~$2,000)

The factory tire, not just the size: your 19" Gemini wheels (the ones on this car) ship on the Continental ProContact RX (255/45R19, 104W XL), extra-load-rated with an acoustic foam inner liner (Continental's ContiSilent) specifically to quiet road noise, which carries further in a car with no engine to mask it. The 20" Induction option instead runs 255/40R20 101W/101Y XL, sourced from either Goodyear (Eagle F1 Asymmetric 5) or Michelin (Pilot Sport EV) depending on the production run. Tesla has used both for the same wheel.

Why the "XL" matters, on this tire or any replacement: EVs run heavier than comparable gas cars because of battery weight, so Tesla specs a minimum 104 load index: an XL (extra load) tire built to carry more at the same pressure than a standard-load tire in the same size. A newer HL (high load) standard exists since 2023, but XL is what your car calls for. Whatever these get replaced with, matching that load rating isn't optional the way tire brand is.

On SKUs: Tesla doesn't publish a public parts catalog the way traditional automakers do, so the "OEM part numbers" quoted on wheel-reseller and salvage listings for a casting like this aren't something Tesla itself puts out. Researching this, we found inconsistent numbers even across listings claiming to describe the same wheel. If you ever need a factory replacement, your VIN plus a Tesla Service Center or Tesla's own parts desk is the reliable path, not a part number copied off a listing site.

If you're shopping replacement tires rather than staying OEM, the common cross-shops in your size (255/45R19) split into three tiers: all-season grand-touring (Michelin CrossClimate2, Continental SecureContact AW, Goodyear Assurance WeatherReady2, Vredestein Quatrac Pro+), ultra-high-performance all-season (Michelin Pilot Sport All Season 4, Continental ExtremeContact DWS 06 Plus, Vredestein Hypertrac All Season), and summer-only performance (Michelin Pilot Sport 4, Bridgestone Potenza Sport, Vredestein Ultrac Pro). None of that is a Tesla endorsement, just what fits and gets cross-shopped in this size. The acoustic foam liner your factory tire has is a comfort nice-to-have you'd lose on most aftermarket choices, not a functional requirement.

Safety & standard equipment

  • NHTSA 5-star overall rating, 5 stars in every sub-category. Tesla's own announcement specifically named the Long Range AWD configuration (your exact trim), with a 7.9% rollover risk it called the lowest of any SUV NHTSA had recorded at the time.
  • Standard: automatic emergency braking, forward collision warning, lane departure warning, blind-spot monitoring, pedestrian AEB, and standard Autopilot (Autosteer + Traffic-Aware Cruise Control).
  • Enhanced Autopilot and Full Self-Driving (Supervised) were separate paid upgrades. More on where your car stands in the Software section below.

What it cost new

Tesla repriced Model Y several times through 2024. A Long Range AWD like yours landed somewhere in the $47,990–$51,990 range before paint, wheels, interior, or FSD. The exact number depends on exactly when in the year it was ordered.
