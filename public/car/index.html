Wheels

Long Range AWD offered exactly two wheel choices in 2024, no 18" or 21" option on this trim (21" Überturbine wheels are Performance-only):

SizeNameTireCost
19"Gemini Wheels255/45R19Standard
20"Induction Wheels255/40R20Paid upgrade (~$2,000)

The factory tire, not just the size: your 19" Gemini wheels (the ones on this car) ship on the Continental ProContact RX (255/45R19, 104W XL), extra-load-rated with an acoustic foam inner liner (Continental's ContiSilent) specifically to quiet road noise, which carries further in a car with no engine to mask it. The 20" Induction option instead runs 255/40R20 101W/101Y XL, sourced from either Goodyear (Eagle F1 Asymmetric 5) or Michelin (Pilot Sport EV) depending on the production run. Tesla has used both for the same wheel.

Why the "XL" matters, on this tire or any replacement: EVs run heavier than comparable gas cars because of battery weight, so Tesla specs a minimum 104 load index: an XL (extra load) tire built to carry more at the same pressure than a standard-load tire in the same size. A newer HL (high load) standard exists since 2023, but XL is what your car calls for. Whatever these get replaced with, matching that load rating isn't optional the way tire brand is.

On SKUs: Tesla doesn't publish a public parts catalog the way traditional automakers do, so the "OEM part numbers" quoted on wheel-reseller and salvage listings for a casting like this aren't something Tesla itself puts out. Researching this, we found inconsistent numbers even across listings claiming to describe the same wheel. If you ever need a factory replacement, your VIN plus a Tesla Service Center or Tesla's own parts desk is the reliable path, not a part number copied off a listing site.

If you're shopping replacement tires rather than staying OEM, the common cross-shops in your size (255/45R19) split into three tiers: all-season grand-touring (Michelin CrossClimate2, Continental SecureContact AW, Goodyear Assurance WeatherReady2, Vredestein Quatrac Pro+), ultra-high-performance all-season (Michelin Pilot Sport All Season 4, Continental ExtremeContact DWS 06 Plus, Vredestein Hypertrac All Season), and summer-only performance (Michelin Pilot Sport 4, Bridgestone Potenza Sport, Vredestein Ultrac Pro). None of that is a Tesla endorsement, just what fits and gets cross-shopped in this size. The acoustic foam liner your factory tire has is a comfort nice-to-have you'd lose on most aftermarket choices, not a functional requirement.

Tesla's refreshed "Juniper" Model Y arrived for the 2025 model year, unveiled January 2025, U.S. deliveries starting March 8, 2025. Your car is a pre-refresh 2024, and the differences are concrete, not just cosmetic buzzwords:

What Juniper changed

  • Full-width LED light bars front and rear, replacing your car's separate headlight/taillight units.
  • New rear-seat touchscreen (8") for climate/media. Your car has no second screen for rear passengers.
  • Removed the gear-selector stalk: Park/Drive/Reverse moved to the touchscreen, similar to Tesla's Model 3 redesign.
  • Kept the physical turn-signal stalk, unlike the Model 3 redesign that eliminated it entirely. Your car and Juniper both still use a real stalk for turn signals.
  • Acoustic glass front and rear for lower wind noise; retuned suspension bushings reviewers describe as noticeably more settled over expansion joints.
  • EPA range rose to roughly 340+ miles, mostly from aerodynamics and efficiency work rather than a bigger battery.
One thing that did not change at launch, worth knowing: Juniper launched with the same 15.4" center screen size your car already has. A larger 16" QHD screen didn't arrive until a June 2026 running change, rolled out gradually, so plenty of early Juniper cars share your exact screen size and resolution.

Charging hardware, NACS port, and Supercharger network access are unchanged between the two generations. Nothing about how you charge today is "outdated."

What this car has, confirmed

  • Autopilot (Autosteer + Traffic-Aware Cruise Control) is standard equipment on every Model Y, including this one, at no extra cost.
  • This car is running FSD (Supervised) v14.3.6, and the $99/month subscription is active on the current owner's account: they added it themselves, so this isn't a leftover trial from a previous owner. "Supervised" is Tesla's own term, meaning the system drives but the driver is required to stay attentive and ready to take over; it is not an unsupervised/robotaxi-grade system on this hardware.
  • The car is also subscribed to Premium Connectivity ($9.99/month, or $99/year): the paid tier above Standard Connectivity's free-for-8-years baseline. It adds satellite-view maps, live traffic visualization, in-car video and music streaming (Netflix, Disney+, Spotify and the like, each still needs its own separate app subscription), an in-car web browser, weather radar, and live Sentry Mode camera viewing from the app when away from the car. It renews next on September 21, 2026.
  • Phone Key is set up: the owner's phone unlocks and starts the car over Bluetooth automatically on approach, no card tap needed. Worth keeping a physical Key Card in the glovebox or a wallet anyway, as backup for the rare time a phone's dead or Bluetooth misbehaves.
  • Software build 2026.20.6.11 is what this car is currently on.
  • Real usage, not a subscription sitting idle: per the Tesla app's Self-Driving Stats, this car is running 81% self-driving on recent trips (64 of the last 80 miles) with a longest single FSD streak of 21.4 miles and a 2-day streak currently active.
Worth knowing for whenever you sell: your FSD subscription is tied to your Tesla account, not the VIN, so it doesn't transfer with the car. If you sell, the subscription simply ends. A buyer who wants FSD starts their own $99/month plan. That's a real difference from the old one-time-purchase model, which Tesla ended entirely on February 14, 2026: FSD bought outright before that cutoff stays with the car, VIN-tied, permanently. Premium Connectivity works the same way: it's a subscription on your account, not an asset that conveys with a sale.

Gone from your car specifically

The Atari arcade classics (Missile Command, Asteroids, Lunar Lander and the rest) were removed in June 2024 specifically from vehicles with AMD Ryzen infotainment. Your 2024 Model Y almost certainly has that hardware, so don't go looking for them; the current Arcade lineup rotates other titles (Cuphead, Beach Buggy Racing 2, Stardew Valley among them).

Also worth correcting: light-up, auto-presenting door handles are a popular aftermarket accessory, not something Tesla actually ships from the factory. If you've seen that on another car, it wasn't stock software.

Things experienced owners find embarrassingly late

  • Trip Planner auto-preconditioning: navigating to a Supercharger automatically warms the battery pack so it hits peak charge speed the moment you plug in, estimated to save up to 10 minutes per stop. A December 2025 update added live stall occupancy to the map.
  • Tesla Theater streaming (Netflix, YouTube, Disney+, Hulu, Twitch): requires the car fully parked with the parking brake set: it does not work while driving, and doesn't turn on just because Autopilot or FSD is engaged, despite that being a common assumption.
  • In-car web browser: a real browser in the app dock, useful at a Supercharger stop. A 2026 update added full-screen support and even browser-based video calling.
  • Naming your car in the app isn't just cosmetic: certain names (Monty Python and Hitchhiker's Guide references among them) trigger their own hidden responses.
Not yet on this car, for what it's worth: the newest door-open "TESLA" wordmark animation that debuted in a mid-2026 software update is limited to 2024+ Model 3 and the redesigned Juniper Model Y. Something to look forward to if you ever upgrade, not something missing from your current software.

What owners buy first

Roughly in the order most owners buy them, based on what comes up most in owner forums and shows up as bestsellers at the stores above:

  • All-weather floor liners: the single most common first purchase. The factory carpet has no protection at all, and liners trimmed to the Model Y footwell keep road salt and mud off it.
  • Frunk and trunk organizers: the car ships with no cargo organization anywhere, front or back. Fitted bins stop groceries and gear from sliding around both trunks.
  • Center console tray: the console's open storage tunnel is deep but unshaped, so small items slide around and disappear into it. A fitted insert fixes that.
  • Windshield and rear sunshades: closer to necessary than optional on a car with this much glass overhead. Interior temperatures climb fast without one.
  • Center screen protector: the 15.4" display handles nearly every control in the car, so it takes constant touch contact. A matte or glass protector is cheap insurance against scratches.
Skip anything that changes the exterior lighting, badging, or aero pieces if resale matters. Those are the easiest changes for a future buyer to flag as not stock, and the cheapest to leave alone.

Footnote: the best selling car claim

Where and when this car's design was built

  • Fremont, CA (your plant): production began January 2020; first deliveries March 13, 2020.
  • Shanghai: production began December 2020.
  • Austin, TX: trial production late 2021; official "Cyber Rodeo" opening April 7, 2022.
  • Fremont built this same body style from January 2020 until the redesigned "Juniper" version arrived in early 2025. Your 2024 car comes from near the very end of that five-year run.
  • Tesla passed 4 million cumulative global Model Y deliveries around March 2026, making it the best-selling EV nameplate in history, independent of any "best overall vehicle" argument.

Was it really the best-selling car in the world?

In 2023, yes, cleanly. JATO Dynamics, an independent auto-data firm, confirmed Model Y as the best-selling vehicle of any kind globally that year: 1.22 million units, ahead of the Toyota RAV4 and Corolla, the first time a pure EV had ever topped that list.

2024 is genuinely disputed, not a clean repeat. JATO's own 2024 count has the Toyota RAV4 narrowly ahead: 1,187,000 to Model Y's 1,185,000, a margin under 3,000 units. A different tracker, Focus2Move, counted Model Y still on top that year. Tesla's own claim of "three years running" (2023–2025) stitches together three different data vendors, one per year. Treat that framing as marketing, not an independently confirmed streak. What's not in dispute: Model Y has been the best-selling EV globally every year since 2023, no asterisk needed.

Awards this exact model year earned

  • IIHS Top Safety Pick+ for 2024, under IIHS's tougher, updated 2023+ criteria that a number of other vehicles failed to meet that cycle. Model Y has now earned this award four years running (2021–2024).
  • Consumer Reports 2024 Top Pick, EV category: the only pure battery-EV on CR's overall Top Picks list that year. Worth noting: CR has historically been one of the tougher graders of Tesla build quality, so this wasn't a rubber stamp.
