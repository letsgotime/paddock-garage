The denominator

A 2024 Tesla Model Y Long Range AWD, financed, charged entirely on Superchargers because the parking situation rules out a home charger. Its running cost is the one number every track has to clear.

Why 13.0¢ is the number that matters. It is the only per-mile figure here derived entirely from receipts. Every alternative on the internet is a national average applied to a car nobody owns. If a track cannot clear its own mileage at 13.0¢, that is a real finding, not a rounding error.

The three tracks

Gig work

Vehicle-dependent by definition, so it carries the full mileage cost. This is the track where the 13.0¢ figure does the most work, because gig pay is quoted gross and the vehicle cost is invisible until you measure it.

One finding already, and it cuts against the common advice: the IRS standard mileage rate near 70¢ is a tax convention built on average drivers, not a cost. At this car's mileage, measured energy plus the mileage-linked share of depreciation puts the true marginal cost near 23¢ a mile, modeled until a second market-value reading confirms the slope. A per-mile rate has to clear that number, not 70¢.

Software

Effectively zero marginal vehicle cost. Four active threads, all recurring-revenue shaped rather than one-time. Three of them are blocked on the same missing asset, which is the sort of thing that only becomes obvious once every thread sits on one board.

Events

Mixed: mostly one-time revenue with real mileage attached, plus one role under discussion that would be recurring. The next milestone is a car show on October 10 at a ranch property outside Nashville, with 31 of 45 vendor booths approved and marketing opening August 30.

What this page is not

It is a summary, not the working system. The tracker underneath holds client names, deal stages, and negotiation detail, none of which belongs on a public page. What appears here is the aggregate: counts, shapes, and figures that describe the work without identifying anyone involved in it.
