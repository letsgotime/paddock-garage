One person, one car, three income tracks: gig work, software, and events. This is the running record of what each of them actually costs to operate and actually returns, built from paid invoices and vehicle telemetry.

Most writing about this stuff runs on estimates. Salary pages publish gross pay and never subtract the vehicle. Cost-per-mile calculators use national averages for a car nobody owns. The rule here is simpler: a number is either measured from a receipt or a sensor, or it is labeled modeled. There is no third category, and the modeled ones get replaced as real data arrives.

The tool

Behind all of this is a private tracker: telemetry, project and income tracking across the three tracks, and the signal work that decides what is worth doing next. It holds contacts and figures, so it sits behind authentication and nothing from it is served publicly. The case study above is the part of it that has been reviewed and approved for publication.

On the numbers. Vehicle figures come from Tesla charging invoices and TezLab telemetry. Income figures are being collected now and are marked as pending until they are real. Nothing here is projected forward to look better than it is.
