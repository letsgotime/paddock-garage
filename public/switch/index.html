The Switch

Going from gas to electric for the first time is a sequence, so it is told as one. Each chapter opens with the data that backs it. The order is the information: this is what happens, roughly in the order it happens to you.

Odometer 20,409.4 mi Per mile 10.1¢ Degradation 0% On FSD 97.1% Snapshot 2026-09-03, baked at deploy. This page makes no live requests.

  1. Chapter 0

    Before

    Three gas vehicles, all of them actually owned and driven, not national averages. A 2015 Express 2500 cargo van doing detail runs, a 2011 Sienna, and a Sequoia that lasted fifteen days. This is the baseline everything else gets measured against.

    • 13 mpg, 28.0¢/mi Chevrolet Express 2500
    • 20 mpg, 18.2¢/mi Toyota Sienna Limited
    • 15 mpg, 24.3¢/mi Toyota Sequoia Platinum 2WD
    • $3.64/gal Pump price

  2. Chapter 1

    The decision

    A used 2024 Model Y Long Range AWD, Solid Black, seven seats and a tow hitch, bought from a Ford Lincoln store in Franklin and financed. Used, because the federal credit was never going to apply to this purchase and someone else had already paid the steepest part of the depreciation curve.

    • 2026-08-22 Delivered
    • 19,4xx mi Odometer at delivery
    • January 2024, Fremont Built
    • 2028 or 50k mi Basic warranty
    • 2032 or 120k mi Battery warranty

  3. Chapter 2

    The first week

    The first Supercharger session, the first time the range number moves faster than expected, and the moment the thing stops feeling like a car and starts feeling like an appliance you plug in. Consumption settled at 270 Wh per mile in mild weather, which is close to the rated figure and better than most owners report.

    • 270 Wh/mi Real consumption
    • 93% Rated efficiency hit
    • 45 mph Average speed

  4. Chapter 3

    Living electric

    9 charging sessions across five locations. Four Tesla Superchargers and one private Level 2 plug that costs nothing to use, which quietly does a lot of work in the cost number. The blended rate is 37.5¢ per kWh. Paying full freight at a Supercharger, it is 42.2¢.

    • 250.2 kWh Energy
    • 37.5¢/kWh Blended
    • 42.2¢/kWh Supercharger only
    • 28 Free kWh

  5. Chapter 4

    FSD

    97.1% of every mile in this window was driven by the car, across 51 of 61 trips. That is the number that changed the commute. A 42 mile run to Franklin is not a 42 mile run any more, it is a 42 mile stretch of doing something else. It still hands control back, and the handoffs are the part nobody puts in the brochure.

    • 898.4 of 924.8 Miles on FSD
    • 97.1% Share
    • 51 of 61 Trips
    • 97% Latest cycle

  6. Chapter 5

    The ledger

    $93.70 of charging bought 925.4 miles. That is 10.1¢ a mile against 28.0¢ for the van, a difference of 17.9¢ on every single mile. TezLab's own savings estimate is more conservative at $42.66, because it compares against a generic car of about 25 mpg rather than the vehicles actually replaced. Both numbers are on the Ledger page.

    • $93.70 Charging spend
    • 10.1¢ Per mile
    • saves 17.9¢/mi Versus the van
    • 9 Sessions

  7. Chapter 6

    The battery

    The question every skeptic asks first. At 20,409 miles and 103 cycles, usable capacity still reads 79 kWh against an original 79 kWh. Zero measured degradation, and both degradation and cycle count land in the low band against comparable cars in the region. One reading is a number. The timeline that makes it a story is being built one Monday at a time.

    • 0% Degradation
    • 79 of 79 kWh Capacity
    • 103 Cycles
    • low on both Versus region
    • 96/100 Range Score

It does not finish

Every Monday the data gets pulled again, compared against what is published here, and any figure that has earned a change gets one. The timeline never closes. That is the product, not a gap in it.

The log

  • 2026-09-03

    First full pull since the site was rebuilt

    Ten days of driving, 61 drives, 925 miles. Four published figures were wrong and have been corrected: cost per mile moved from 13.0¢ to 10.1¢ as cheaper sessions and one free plug entered the average, the blended energy rate fell from 42.9¢ to 37.5¢ a kWh, battery cycles went from 100 to 103, and the odometer from 19,748 to 20,409. The 9.4% charge loss figure was withdrawn rather than restated, because this pull does not return the fields needed to recompute it.
