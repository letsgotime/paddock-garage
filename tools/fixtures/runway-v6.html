Tactics: tips and time on the clock

Field-tested tactics from shopper guides, grouped by where they save time or move the tip.

Shop faster

  • Work Kroger, Publix, Sprouts, and Aldi only, four stores you know cold beats six you're still learning
  • Scan the full list before you grab a cart, plan the route by aisle instead of chasing items in order
  • Cold items last: produce and pantry first, frozen and dairy right before checkout
  • Place any deli order first, then keep shopping, come back when it's ready instead of standing in line
  • On a "can't find," tap it and move on. Most customers have already picked their replacement preference in the app, don't spend the minute deciding for them
  • Bag produce while you're standing in the checkout line, not after
  • Park next to the cart corral, not the entrance, it costs nothing and saves a minute every single batch

Win the tip

  • Send one message at the start: who you are and that you're shopping now. Customers tip the shopper they can picture, not a faceless order number
  • Text before substituting, not after. A quick heads-up beats a surprise at the door every time
  • Arrive dressed like you're going to work, not like you just rolled out of bed. It reads in the first three seconds at the door
  • Offer to carry heavy items past the threshold instead of leaving them on the porch
  • Keep a couple of pre-written message templates in your phone's notes app for arrival, substitutions, and delays, so a fast reply never costs you shopping time
Once the first 10 batches and your rating are solid: pick up double batches from the same store to cut combined shop time, keep a photo timestamp of every delivered order as theft-claim protection, and run grocery cashback apps (Fetch, Ibotta) on your own receipts as a small side channel that costs nothing extra.

DoorDash plays by different rules. Dasher Rewards gates priority on acceptance rate, not just completion: Platinum needs 70% acceptance, 95% completion, a 4.7 rating, and 100 deliveries in the last 30 days. On Instacart, declining a weak batch costs nothing but time. On DoorDash, declining too often costs the tier itself. Don't run the Batch Evaluator's decline-freely habit over onto this app without adjusting for that.

Advertising & audience

Wearables and leave-behinds, weighed against what's documented.

ChannelWhat's documentedVerdict
Hat or shirt, identity onlyInstacart has no dress code and offers no official branded gear, shoppers have full attire freedomWear it
Card or flyer left in the orderNot confirmed either way, Instacart's shopper terms and community guidelines pages block automated access. Every major gig platform's general pattern restricts off-platform solicitation of paying customersSkip
Car exterior signage or magnetsUber and Lyft explicitly ban this and enforce it, one driver on record was penalized at an airport for a forgotten magnet. Instacart's exact clause wasn't accessible to confirm, but the pattern holdsSkip
Why the hat clears and the card doesn't. Clothing is yours, worn, not handed to anyone, not inserted into a paid order, nothing to solicit. A card or flyer physically joins the delivery a customer paid Instacart for, and if it's ever flagged, that complaint lands on the one number that controls your batch access. Keep the wearable to identity only, wordmark and garage.paddock20.com, no phone number, no "hire me" copy, so it reads as apparel, not a sign.
The hat does the leave-behind's job without the risk. Same silent impression, every single delivery, zero insertion into the order, zero solicitation because it's not directed at anyone, it's just what you're wearing when you knock. It also costs about $20 once and shows up in every blog and LinkedIn photo from here forward, worth doing even though most doorsteps aren't Paddock20's buyer, because the downside is nothing and it compounds with the build-in-public content from the shift log.
If a customer asks, answer. Don't lead. Someone clocks the hat or the Model Y and asks what you do, that's an opening, not a solicitation, you didn't start it. Mention garage.paddock20.com if it fits the conversation. That's the entire allowance: reactive, brief, never initiated at the door.

    Booked through Paddock20

    What "through the business" means in practice, since Instacart itself only contracts with individuals.

    • Instacart runs its background check and 1099-NEC on you personally, by Social Security number. There's no shopper account type for a business entity
    • "Through Paddock20" means the payouts land in a Paddock20 business account and get booked on Paddock20's ledger as gig revenue, with the mileage and shift costs claimed against it. The account and the tax filing sit under the legal entity behind Paddock20, the entity
    • If the entity is a single-member LLC taxed as a disregarded entity, this still flows to your personal Schedule C under its EIN. If it has an S-corp election, the mechanics change. Confirm which applies with your CPA before your first payout, not after
